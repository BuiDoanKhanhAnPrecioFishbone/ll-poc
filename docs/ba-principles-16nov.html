VIQ UI Principles, 16 Nov 2026 — checked against this system

The BA's settled answers to the open design questions, received as VIQ_UI_Principles_26Nov16.html and compared value by value against src/theme/ and the pages here.

The headline: it agrees with us almost everywhere. It uses our token names (--vy-danger, space-1…14, control-md, col-money), and where both documents state a number they match on 31 of the 38 checked. That is not a coincidence — it is the same system written from the other side.

Most of it is now adopted — the status palette, the sizes and the scroll bars. Four things are flagged below, because this system holds a measurement that disagrees, and a measurement is worth raising before it is overwritten.

1. The date-time FORMAT, now that the width is settled

Adopting her widths surfaced a difference the widths were hiding. Both examples are 19 characters, so 170px fits either — but they are not the same format:

Example

The date-only format matches exactly. Only the time part differs, and her own list still carries date format as unresolved, so this is the question to close next: 12-hour without seconds, or 24-hour with them?

2. Which Kendo step control-row sits on

She writes control-row 26px … (Kendo sm). This system's sizing page says the same 26px is Kendo's xs step. The value agrees; the name of the rung does not, and one of the two is wrong in a way that will mislead whoever next configures the Kendo size scale.

One difference that is not a conflict. Her type scale gives 2xs 10px and xs 11px and says they are "not used on desktop, below the 12px floor"; ours sets both to 12px outright. Same result on screen, and hers states the reason better.

Status words. She names seven business statuses — New · In Progress · Quoted · Waiting for Approval · Completed · On Hold · Cancelled. This system has eight --vy-status-* token pairs. These are different lists, not a disagreement: hers is the vocabulary a user reads, ours is the palette it is drawn from. It also explains a confusion already recorded here — Badge.tsx says "seven-token vocabulary" while B1.6 says "eight statuses", and both are right about different things. Worth asking her which token each of the seven words maps to, and writing that mapping down.

Flagged: the priority dot

Hers is High #C62D26 red · Medium #C27A12 amber · Low #8A94A6 grey. This system draws three steps of one ink — solid, half, hollow — and the reason is in the code:

High was red and Medium amber, which borrowed the two colours this product uses for "something is wrong" and spent them on a CATEGORY. A high-priority RFQ is not an error; an overdue one is, and on a list that shows both the reader had no way to tell the two reds apart.

That change was made on the stated grounds that "the standard never mentions Priority, so the palette was ours to choose". Her document now does mention it, so that premise is gone — but the collision it describes is not. Under her palette a High priority dot is red and a blocked status pill is red, on the same row.

Question for her: on a list showing Priority and Status together, how should a reader tell a high-priority row from a blocked one?

Third pass — Input widgets

Her largest section turned out to be far smaller than its size suggested. 10,100 characters and 180 px values, and only four colour values in it were new to this system: the rest name ramp values we already had under our own names — her border-input is our grey-400, her focus is blue-500, her text-label is grey-600, her changed-border is the brand.

Five tokens added, and wired: --vy-value-box and --vy-value-box-line (the read-only value box, which this system already drew but with borrowed tokens), --vy-changed-border, --vy-disabled-bg and --vy-disabled-text. contrast:check now measures 188 pairs rather than 184.

One number she should see. Her disabled pair measures 2.47:1 in light and 2.95:1 in dark. WCAG 1.4.3 exempts an inactive control, so this is permitted and the check declares it rather than failing — but a disabled label at 2.47:1 is hard to read for more people than the exemption implies, and some teams deliberately exceed it. The figure is now printed by contrast:check on every run rather than buried.

Two Kendo traps, both already written down here, both walked into anyway. The disabled rule had to be prefixed html[data-kendo-bridged] to outweigh the bridge, and it had to target .k-disabled — Kendo disables with a class, not the :disabled attribute, and a Button never carries .vy-btn at all. The first two versions of the rule matched nothing and the button stayed brand blue. Only opening it in a browser showed that.
