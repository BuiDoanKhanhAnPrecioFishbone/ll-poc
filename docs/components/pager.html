Pager

src/ui/Pager.tsx · at the foot of every grid.

When to use

At the foot of a grid, always. It carries both the position and the record count.

When not to use

Instead of the pagerUseWhy
A list that is always shortNothingA pager over one page is furniture
A feed read from newest downInfinite scrollA feed has no "page 7" anyone wants
Showing the total somewhere elseThe pagerThe 25 Aug review moved the count out of the page title and into here, on purpose

Anatomy

PartRule
Count1 - 20 of 66 items, worded as the live pager words it. No items when empty
Page size20 · 50 · 100 — exactly what the live pager offers. It was 25/50/100 here and was wrong
Nav« ‹ · numbers · › », in a <nav aria-label="Pagination">

Page size lives in the pager, not the toolbar, because it is a property of the pager and means nothing without it.

First and last are not optional. The guideline requires "the user can navigate to the first, previous, next, and last page", each disabled at its own end. Without them, reaching the end of 17 pages is 17 clicks.

The number window

Up to 7 pages, every number is shown. Past that: first, a window of three around the current page, last, with … for the gaps.

330 records at 25 a page is 14 buttons, which is a row of numbers nobody reads. Keeping first and last visible means jump to the end stays one click, which is the only long-distance move people actually make.

Measured on Project Requirements: 66 items at 20 a page = 4 pages, so all four numbers show and there are zero gaps — the window only appears when it earns its place.

What it replaced, and what that cost

It replaces infinite scroll over a virtualised list, and it is worth being straight that the old one was faster: the previous grid rendered about 40 DOM nodes for 21,941 records and never paged.

The trade is deliberate. A pager gives a position. Scrolling tells you there is more; a pager tells you where you are, lets you come back to where you were, and is how everyone on this system already reads a long list.

Virtualisation stays underneath, so a 200-row page is still cheap.
