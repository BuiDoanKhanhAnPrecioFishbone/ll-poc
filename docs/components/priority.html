Priority

src/ui/Priority.tsx · 29 lines, the smallest component here.

When to use

Wherever a record's priority is shown — a grid column, a record header, a queue card.

When not to use

Instead of PriorityUseWhy
A workflow stateStatus badgePriority is how urgent; status is where it is
Letting the user set itA selectThis renders a value. It is output
A severity that came from the system, not a personA row tone or dangerPriority is somebody's judgement about a record

Anatomy

A dot and a word. The column is 104px (B5.3) — a dot, a gap and six characters, which is the widest word, Medium.

LevelDotText
HighFilled, --vy-on-surface-strong--vy-weight-medium
MediumFilled, --vy-on-surface-faintdefault
LowHollow — a 1.5px inset ring in --vy-border-mid--vy-on-surface-muted

Low is a hollow dot, not a pale one. On a grid a light fill and an empty cell look the same, and no priority set is a different fact from low priority. The ring says there is a value here.

It was three stars, and the change is the point

The 25 August review asked for a dot plus a word. The reasoning holds and is worth keeping where someone will find it:

  • A star rating is an input idiom. It says rate this. On a read-only row it invites a click that does nothing.
  • It makes the reader count. Two filled stars out of three is a fraction the eye has to resolve before it means anything — and it means nothing at all unless you already know the scale runs to three.
  • ***Medium* needs no scale and no counting.**

The dot keeps the colour, so a row can still be scanned without reading the words.

It encodes by ink, not by hue. High is near-black, Medium is mid-grey, Low is an outline. Printed, photocopied or seen by someone who cannot separate hues, the three levels still rank themselves — which is more than the status vocabulary can claim, and it is why priority does not borrow the accent colours.

Content

Three words only: High · Medium · Low. The live record stores 1–3, and 3 is most urgent — the mapping is in priorityLevel() and nowhere else, so a screen cannot invent a fourth level or flip the direction.

Behaviour

None. It is not focusable and not operable.
