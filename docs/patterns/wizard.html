Pattern · Wizard

A long job split into steps you can walk back through. Run Quotation is the only one in the product, and it is four steps deep. Components: Stepper · Button · Dialog · FileDrop

The shape

BandRule
1TitleThe job and the record: Run Quotation — RFQ0000000358
2SubtitleWhat this step is for, plus the facts that do not change between steps
3Nav + stepperPrevious · the step's own button on the left; the stepper on the right
4Step bodyWhatever this step is

The dialog is xl and opens maximised: a 21-column grid in a 1180px window spends the screen it was given.

The steps are buttons

Not decoration. Someone who realises on step 3 that they mis-mapped a column on step 1 should go straight back to it, not press Back twice and lose their place.

Forward movement is bounded by furthest — the highest step reached so far. You can move freely across work you have already done; you cannot skip ahead into a step whose inputs do not exist yet.

A stepper that lets you land on Summary before a BoM is uploaded is offering a dead end.

One filled button per step

Before this rule, step 3 showed two — Get Prices in its toolbar and Generate Quotation in the nav — so the screen offered two answers to "what now".

The rule, from the Standard, stated exactly:

If the step's main action is not done yet, that action is primary and Next turns secondary but stays enabled.

Stays enabled is the load-bearing half. A quote with no prices in it is a legitimate thing to carry forward, and the reason not to is better said by a tooltip than by a dead control — "Get prices first, or continue with a quote that has none".

No Cancel. The dialog's own close button is the way out, as in the customer's mockup. A second one in a row of four would be the only control here that does not move the wizard forward or back.

The button is named after its result

Step 3's button says Next, not Generate Quotation.

The press that actually writes is this one: it sets NO BID on the supplier-less lines and moves to step 4. A button that generates nothing should not be named after generating something — the rule is that a button names its result, and the result here is step 4. The quotation is created by Submit, on the last step, which is what it is called.

Two popups deliberately not built

The user manual of 29 October describes two confirmations this build does not have. Both omissions are decisions, recorded on 29 Oct so they can be revisited.

The manualHereWhy
Step 2 → NextA Review Excluded Parts popup listing excluded parts, with tick boxes to put them backNo popup. Excluding and restoring happens on the step-2 grid, and an Excluded Only filter — now with a count — opens that exact list at any timeThe popup's content is a list the screen behind it is already showing, and the fix lives on the grid. The popup only adds a press between the user and the place they edit
Step 3 → NextA Confirm Importing BoM popup listing supplier-less and excess parts with a Total Excess AmountNo popup. Those numbers print on the bar beside NextA modal asking "are you sure" about four numbers is a modal that should have printed the four numbers. The user still reads the consequence before pressing — without losing a beat
The fourth number was missing, and holding the two side by side found it. The line beside Next was printing three of the popup's four facts; Total Excess Amount was added on 30 Oct. What would change this decision: a real user pressing Next without reading that line — somebody submitting a quote containing NO BID without knowing. Then the argument is wrong and the popup comes back. Worth watching in a session with real users.

Responsive

The stepper is where this pattern meets the layout, and the breakpoints were measured rather than guessed:

WidthWhat changesWhy
≤ 840pxStep descriptions goFour labelled steps need about 760px of stepper; below that the labels truncate, and a truncated "1 - Confi…" is worse than no description — the step name is what you navigate by
≤ 700pxOnly the current step keeps its label; the rest become their marker"2 - Review BoM" needs 98px of label and a step costs about 146px with its marker, gap and padding — four of those is 584px. The markers still show position and stay clickable

Accessibility

StepsEach states its own status to a screen reader, so where am I and what is left does not depend on seeing the ticks
Unreachable stepsDisabled, not hidden — the shape of the job stays visible
Numberingnumbered={false} where the labels already carry numbers, or every step reads "① 1 - …", which looks like a rendering fault
BusySubmit disables while writing; a second press would submit twice
